# Ordinary Life Takes the Stage
slug: ordinary-life-takes-the-stage
#art_history #painting #realism #everyday_life #bruegel #impressionism
level: standard: curious adults of better-than-average ability with no art-history background; define a term once, in passing
language: English
notation: Each lecture is one painting shown large as a mapped picture: soft lights, zooms and arrows on its parts; strength/rest emphasis (grey, blur, paper) at one or two moments. Narrator: one voice, a warm gallery guide.
example: The running thread is who gets to be the subject of a big painting: from a ploughman who pushes a myth into the corner to a whole park of Parisians on their day off. Each lecture asks what is ordinary in the picture, why painting it was new or daring then, and how the painter makes the everyday worth looking at. Lectures may refer back to earlier paintings in a phrase, never a recap.

Ten paintings from 1560 to 1886 in which ordinary people and everyday work move from the edge of the picture to its centre — and why that was once a provocation.
