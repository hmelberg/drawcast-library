# The Painting Knows You're There
slug: the-painting-knows-youre-there
#art_history #painting #renaissance_art #perspective #symbolism
level: standard: curious adults of better-than-average ability with no art-history background; define a term once, in passing
language: English
notation: Each lecture is one painting shown large as a mapped picture: soft lights, zooms and arrows on its parts; strength/rest emphasis (grey, blur, paper) at one or two moments. Narrator: one voice, a warm gallery guide.
example: The running thread is the viewer's own place: each lecture adds one way a painting plays with where you stand and what you notice (a mirror, a viewpoint, a hidden subject, a pointing light, a mirror that shows you, a curtain, a hidden watcher, a back turned to you, a street that walks toward you) — and Manet's mirror at the end uses them all. Lectures may refer back to earlier paintings in a phrase, never a recap.

Ten paintings from 1434 to 1882 that know someone is looking — mirrors, impossible viewpoints, hidden watchers — and what each one does with you, the viewer.
