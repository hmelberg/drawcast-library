# How Painters Caught Light
slug: how-painters-caught-light
level: standard: curious adults of better-than-average ability with no art-history background; define a term once, in passing
language: English
notation: Each lecture is one painting shown large as a mapped picture, explained as a conversation between two voices: a = the guide (Gemini Charon, warm and knowledgeable), b = a curious visitor (Gemini Puck, relaxed, good-humoured, asks, guesses, sometimes wrong, sums up). Soft lights on the picture are the natural tool here; strength/rest emphasis (grey, blur, paper) at one or two moments where the light IS the point.
example: The running thread is one question asked of every painting: where does the light come from, and what is it doing for the story? Each lecture adds one way of catching light — oil glazes, a single beam, light picking out a hero, a candle, window light, a sunset, light instead of things, the same stone at different hours, light built from dots, light as feeling. Lectures may refer back to earlier paintings in a phrase, never a recap.

Ten paintings from 1434 to 1889, each a new answer to one question: how do you put light on a flat surface? Told as a conversation between a guide and a curious visitor.
