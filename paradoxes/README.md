# Twelve Paradoxes That Broke the Rules

Twelve arguments where every step looks fine and the conclusion is impossible — from Zeno's tortoise to Arrow's voting theorem — and what finding the broken step taught mathematics, logic and decision-making.

1. [How can Achilles ever catch the tortoise? Zeno's paradox](https://www.drawcast.app/#gh=hmelberg/drawcast-library/paradoxes/01-how-can-achilles-ever-catch-the-tortoise.cast)
   - If every gap takes time to close, how can infinitely many gaps take finite time?
   - Why did it take until the 1800s to say what an infinite sum means?
2. [Can a full hotel take one more guest? Hilbert's Hotel and Cantor's diagonal](https://www.drawcast.app/#gh=hmelberg/drawcast-library/paradoxes/02-can-a-full-hotel-take-one-more-guest.cast)
   - How can a full infinite hotel make room for a busload of new guests?
   - Why can't any list contain every decimal number?
   - What does it mean for one infinity to be bigger than another?
3. [Can one ball become two? The Banach–Tarski paradox](https://www.drawcast.app/#gh=hmelberg/drawcast-library/paradoxes/03-can-one-ball-become-two-the-banach.cast)
   - How can cutting and moving pieces double a ball?
   - Why can't the pieces have a volume?
   - Does this say something about the world, or about the axiom of choice?
4. [Does the set of all sets that don't contain themselves contain itself? Russell's paradox](https://www.drawcast.app/#gh=hmelberg/drawcast-library/paradoxes/04-does-the-set-of-all-sets-that-don-t.cast)
   - Why does a harmless-looking rule for making sets lead to a contradiction?
   - What did Russell's 1902 letter do to Frege's life's work?
   - How did mathematics rebuild set theory so the paradox can't be asked?
5. [What is the smallest number you can't describe in a few words? Berry's paradox](https://www.drawcast.app/#gh=hmelberg/drawcast-library/paradoxes/05-what-is-the-smallest-number-you-can-t.cast)
   - Why does 'the smallest number not nameable in under sixty letters' name one in under sixty letters?
   - How did Chaitin turn this into a proof that most truths about randomness can't be proved?
6. [Can maths prove everything? Gödel's incompleteness theorems](https://www.drawcast.app/#gh=hmelberg/drawcast-library/paradoxes/06-can-maths-prove-everything-godel-s.cast)
   - How can a sentence about numbers say 'I have no proof'?
   - Why can't adding the missing truth as an axiom fix it?
7. [Can a program tell whether any program will finish? Turing's halting problem](https://www.drawcast.app/#gh=hmelberg/drawcast-library/paradoxes/07-can-a-program-tell-whether-any-program.cast)
   - Why would a perfect bug-checker contradict itself when run on itself?
   - What does this mean for real software and real computers?
8. [Can a surprise be announced? The surprise exam paradox](https://www.drawcast.app/#gh=hmelberg/drawcast-library/paradoxes/08-can-a-surprise-be-announced-the-surprise.cast)
   - Why does the students' proof that the exam can't happen seem airtight?
   - Where exactly does the reasoning go wrong — and do logicians agree?
9. [When does sand become a heap? The sorites paradox](https://www.drawcast.app/#gh=hmelberg/drawcast-library/paradoxes/09-when-does-sand-become-a-heap-the-sorites.cast)
   - If one grain never makes the difference, how do heaps exist?
   - Is the boundary sharp but unknown, blurred, or not there at all?
10. [Would you pay a fortune for an infinitely valuable game? The St Petersburg paradox](https://www.drawcast.app/#gh=hmelberg/drawcast-library/paradoxes/10-would-you-pay-a-fortune-for-an.cast)
   - Why is the expected payout of a simple coin game infinite?
   - Why will nobody pay much to play — and what did Bernoulli conclude about utility?
11. [One box or two? Newcomb's problem](https://www.drawcast.app/#gh=hmelberg/drawcast-library/paradoxes/11-one-box-or-two-newcomb-s-problem.cast)
   - Why do two airtight arguments recommend opposite choices?
   - What does the split between one-boxers and two-boxers say about how we decide?
12. [Can any voting rule be fair? Arrow's impossibility theorem](https://www.drawcast.app/#gh=hmelberg/drawcast-library/paradoxes/12-can-any-voting-rule-be-fair-arrow-s.cast)
   - Why can a majority prefer A to B, B to C and C to A?
   - Why must every fair-looking voting rule break one reasonable condition?
