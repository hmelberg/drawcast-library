Which ramp is fastest?

A straight ramp, a steep drop, or a swooping curve: tap the one that wins the race, then hear how Newton solved it overnight.

Three ramps join the same two points. Which one gets a ball to the bottom first?

One is straight, one swoops down in a curve, and one drops steeply, then runs flat.

Which ramp gets a rolling ball from start to finish fastest? Tap it.

The curve. It dips first, so the ball gains speed early, and that speed carries it the rest of the way.

Race them. The straight line is the shortest path, but the ball gathers speed slowly.

The steep drop builds speed fast, then spends it on a long flat run.

The curve is steep at first, then swoops through and beats them both.

It even dips below the finish and climbs the last stretch: the speed it gains early is worth the climb.

That curve is a cycloid: the path traced by a point on the rim of a rolling wheel.

In 1696 Johann Bernoulli challenged the best mathematicians of Europe to find this fastest path.

Isaac Newton got the problem one afternoon in January 1697, after a day at the Royal Mint. By four the next morning, he had solved it.

He sent his answer without a name. But Bernoulli knew at once who it was: you can tell the lion by its claw.
