The deadliest animal

Guess which animals kill the most people each year, and see why the deadliest are the ones that carry disease, not the ones with teeth.

Which animal kills the most people? What we fear and what actually kills us turn out to be very different things. Here are four suspects.

Rank them: which kills the most people each year?

Let's count the deaths in a year. Sharks, the villains of a hundred films: fewer than ten. The bar barely leaves the floor.

Hippos kill around five hundred, crocodiles about a thousand. Huge, fierce, and still almost invisible here.

Dogs kill far more: nearly sixty thousand a year.

Snakes: around a hundred thousand, mostly farmers bitten far from any hospital with antivenom.

Now the mosquito. How many people does it kill a year? Drag its bar to your guess.

Six hundred thousand, from malaria alone. The smallest suspect on your list outkills every predator on this chart, many times over.

Yet the bite itself is harmless. What kills is the malaria parasite, slipped into your blood with the mosquito's saliva as it drinks.

And the dogs? Mostly not their teeth: rabies, a virus passed in a bite, and almost always fatal once symptoms begin.

So the deadliest animals are not the strongest, but the ones that carry disease.

One bar is still missing, the runner-up: us. Murder alone kills about four hundred and forty thousand people a year, and wars add more.

So fear sharks if you must. But the animal worth fighting is the one you swat, which is why bed nets and malaria drugs save more lives than any shark net.
