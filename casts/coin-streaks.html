You toss a fair coin five times, and it lands heads every time. What comes next?

Tails, obviously. It's overdue!

After five heads in a row, what is the chance the next toss is tails?

Exactly half. The coin has no memory: every toss starts from scratch.

But five heads in a row is rare. Doesn't that count for something?

It is rare: one chance in thirty-two.

But those five are already done. Only the next toss is still open, and it is a plain coin toss.

Tell that to a casino.

Funny you should say that. In Monte Carlo, in August 1913, a roulette ball landed on black twenty-six times in a row.

Gamblers piled their money on red, sure it was due. They lost millions.

So the gambler's fallacy has a home address.

It does. It's often called the Monte Carlo fallacy, and the wheel never knew it was on a streak.
