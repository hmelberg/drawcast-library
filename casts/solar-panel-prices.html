How fast did solar panels get cheaper?

Draw where the price of solar panels went after 2008, then see the crash: from $132 a watt in 1975 to about 27 cents today.

How fast have solar panels become cheaper? Here is the price of one watt of panel, in today's dollars.

In 2000 a watt cost about six and a half dollars. By 2008 it was still almost five. What happened next? Draw the line on to 2024.

That is a fall of about ninety-four percent in sixteen years, to twenty-seven cents.

After 2008, giant factories, many of them in China, pushed the price down faster than ever.

The rule of thumb, called Swanson's law: every doubling of all the panels ever made cuts the price by about a fifth.

That is why the International Energy Agency calls solar some of the cheapest electricity ever seen.

And the story starts earlier. In 1975, the same watt cost about a hundred and thirty-two dollars, far off the top of this chart.

From then to now, the price has fallen by more than ninety-nine percent.
