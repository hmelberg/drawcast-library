My family in Russian: мой, моя́, моё

Say my mum, my dad and my sister in Russian, and see why my has three forms, мой, моя́ and моё, that follow the noun's ending.

How do you say my in Russian? Masha's family photos will show you: it isn't one word.

Э́то means this is, and it brings a new letter, Э, which says e, as in end; not Е, which says ye.

Now listen to my: моя́ ма́ма, but мой па́па. Two different words for my.

So why мой брат, but моя́ сестра́? Bring back lesson two's three groups: он, она́ and оно́.

Брат, brother, starts with a new letter, Б, which says b; it ends in a consonant, like кот, so both are он.

Ма́ма and сестра́, sister, end in а, so they are она́.

And two things in Masha's home are оно́: окно́, a window, and молоко́, milk.

Now each group gets its own my: он words take мой, она́ words take моя́, and оно́ words take моё.

Listen to the ends: моя́ ма́ма, моё окно́. My picks up the noun's own ending, and мой ends in a consonant, й, like брат and кот.

So which my does па́па take? He ends in а, like ма́ма.

But a dad is a he, so it's мой па́па: as in lesson two, the meaning wins over the ending.

Put э́то in front, and you can introduce anyone: э́то моя́ ма́ма, this is my mum. Э́то never changes; only my does.

Five questions now, then your score. Put each word under its my: мой, моя́ or моё.

Masha points at her sister. Э́то ___ сестра́. Which word fills the gap?

Моя́: сестра́ ends in а, so моя́ сестра́.

And her dad: Э́то ___ па́па.

Мой: па́па ends in а, but a dad is он, so мой па́па.

Type my for a window: ___ окно́.

Моё: окно́ ends in о, so моё окно́.

What does Э́то мой брат mean?

This is my brother: э́то, this is; мой брат, my brother.

You got 5 of 5.

So my in Russian copies its noun: мой for a he, моя́ for a she, моё for an it, and the noun's last letter tells you which, as in lesson two.
