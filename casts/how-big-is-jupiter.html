How big is Jupiter?

The planets drawn at their true sizes: how many Earths fit inside Jupiter, and why it weighs far less than its size suggests.

How big is Jupiter, really? Here the planets are drawn at their true sizes, and the Sun is so large that only its edge fits.

Come closer. Earth is 12,700 kilometres across: a dot beside a Sun that is 109 Earths wide.

The other rocky planets are smaller still: Venus nearly Earth's twin, Mars half as wide, Mercury less.

And then Jupiter: 143,000 kilometres across. How many Earths is that?

Line Earths up across its middle, and eleven fit side by side.

But eleven times wider is also eleven times taller and eleven times deeper, so about 1,300 Earths would fit inside it.

Yet it weighs only 318 Earths. It is mostly hydrogen and helium, a quarter as dense as our rock and iron.

Even so, it is two and a half times as heavy as all the other planets put together.

That is the giant. Now clear the Earths away and step back out.

Saturn, nine and a half Earths across, is lighter still: less dense than water.

Then come the two ice giants, Uranus and Neptune, each about four Earths across.

Uranus is four Earths across. About how many Earths would fit inside it?

About 64: four times wider, four times taller and four times deeper, four cubed.
