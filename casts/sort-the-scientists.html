Who discovered what? Match four scientists

Match Harvey, Faraday, Noether and Meitner to what they discovered, and hear how Lise Meitner missed a Nobel but got an element.

Four scientists, four discoveries. Some of these names you know; some you should. Who found what?

Match each scientist to their discovery: drag from one card to its partner.

William Harvey, an English doctor, showed in 1628 that the heart pumps the same blood round and round the body.

Michael Faraday, a blacksmith's son, built the first electric motor in 1821: a wire that spun round a magnet.

Emmy Noether proved in 1918 that every symmetry in the laws of nature comes with something conserved, such as energy.

Einstein called her a creative mathematical genius.

And Lise Meitner, with her nephew Otto Frisch, explained nuclear fission at the end of 1938: an atom's core splitting in two.

But the Nobel Prize for it went to her colleague Otto Hahn alone.

Science made amends later: element 109 is named meitnerium, after her.
