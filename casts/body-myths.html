Body myths: true or myth?

Five things people say about your body, from cracked knuckles to swallowed spiders: true or myth? Guess each one, then hear the story behind it.

Your body, true or myth? First: cracking your knuckles gives you arthritis.

Cracking your knuckles gives you arthritis. True or myth?

Myth. A doctor, Donald Unger, cracked the knuckles of one hand for sixty years and never the other: no arthritis in either, and an Ig Nobel Prize.

Next, the one that ruins bedtime: we all swallow spiders in our sleep.

We swallow spiders in our sleep. True or myth?

Myth. No case has ever been recorded, and a big, breathing, rumbling sleeper is the last place a spider wants to go.

Then the razor: shaved hair grows back thicker.

Shaved hair grows back thicker. True or myth?

Myth. The razor leaves a blunt tip that feels coarse, but a study in 1928 already found the hair grows back no thicker and no faster.

Now a school lesson: you have more than five senses.

Humans have more than five senses. True or myth?

True. Balance, heat, pain, and knowing where your arms are with your eyes shut are senses too: scientists count far more than five.

And last: your stomach gets a new lining every few days.

Your stomach lining renews itself every few days. True or myth?

True. The cells on the stomach's surface are replaced every three to five days, before its own acid can digest them.

You got 5 of 5.

The three family warnings were myths. The two from biology class were true.
