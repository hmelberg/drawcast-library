How old is the typical person? Niger, Brazil, the US, Italy and Japan

Place five countries on a line by the age of their middle person, then see why Japan, the oldest, was younger in 1950 than Brazil is today.

How old is the typical person in a country? Line everyone up by age and take the one in the middle: that is the median age.

Here are five countries, from Africa, South America, North America, Europe and Asia.

Drag each country to its median age on the line.

Here are the true ages: from about fifteen in Niger to forty-nine in Japan.

Niger is one of the youngest countries on Earth: half its people are children.

So Japan's middle person is thirty-four years older than Niger's.

And the whole world sits between them, at about thirty.

But Japan was not always old. In 1950, its median age was twenty-one.

That is younger than Brazil is today.

Fewer babies and much longer lives aged Japan by nearly thirty years in seven decades.

Niger is on the same road, only later: the UN expects its median age to pass thirty around 2082.

So one of the youngest countries today may one day be as old as the whole world is now.
