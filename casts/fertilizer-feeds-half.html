Imagine these hundred people stand for everyone alive. How many of them owe their food to fertilizer made in a factory?

Sweep across the people it feeds.

About forty-eight in a hundred: across the world, roughly four billion people.

Half of us? Fed by a sack of fertilizer?

By the nitrogen in it. Crops need nitrogen, and the air is mostly nitrogen, but in a form no plant can use.

In 1909 the German chemist Fritz Haber found how to pull it out of the air as ammonia, and by 1913 Carl Bosch had it running in a factory.

So half of us eat bread made from thin air.

Pretty much. Haber won the Nobel Prize for it in 1918.

Wait, Haber? Isn't he the man behind the poison gas of the First World War?

He is. He oversaw the first chlorine gas attack, at Ypres in 1915.

The father of chemical warfare, and the reason billions of people are fed. Quite a legacy.
