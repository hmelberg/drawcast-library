True or myth? Five things everyone has heard

Five things almost everyone has heard, from goldfish memory to radioactive bananas: true or myth? Guess each, then find out why.

Five things almost everyone has heard. For each one: true, or myth?

First: you can see the Great Wall of China from space.

The Great Wall can be seen from space with the naked eye. True or myth?

Myth. The wall is long but only about as wide as a road, and China's first astronaut, Yang Liwei, could not see it in 2003.

Next, the goldfish, said to forget everything in three seconds.

A goldfish's memory lasts only about three seconds. True or myth?

Myth. Goldfish remember for months: they learn when food comes, and which sound means dinner.

Then the old saying that lightning never strikes the same place twice.

Lightning never strikes the same place twice. True or myth?

Myth. Tall things get hit again and again: the Empire State Building is struck about twenty-five times a year.

Now the breakfast table: bananas are said to be radioactive.

Bananas are radioactive. True or myth?

True. A tiny share of their potassium is radioactive potassium-40, though your body keeps its potassium level steady, so it does no harm.

And last, the octopus, said to have three hearts.

An octopus has three hearts. True or myth?

True. Two hearts pump blood through the gills, and a third sends it round the body.

You got 5 of 5.

And the two that sounded silliest were true.
