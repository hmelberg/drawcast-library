One line or three?

One shared line beats a line per till: a slow customer holds up one till, not everyone behind them.

Why do some shops make everyone wait in one long line? Picture three tills.

Nine shoppers arrive, and each picks a till and queues behind it.

Then the shopper at till two needs a price check.

Tills one and three keep serving, and empty, while the two people behind her stand still.

Now take the same shoppers and tills, but one shared line.

Whoever is at the front goes to the next free till.

The price check still holds up one till, but the line flows past it to the other two.

Nobody gets stuck behind someone else's bad luck, so the wait is shorter on average, and fairer.

A bank has four tellers, and one customer takes ages. Which setup keeps everyone else moving?

One shared line: the slow customer ties up one teller, and the line flows on to the other three.

That is why banks and airports wind one long line through the hall.
