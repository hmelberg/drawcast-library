Which is more common among the world's adults: being too thin, or having obesity? Two rounds, thirty-four years apart.

Tap the bigger share in each pair.

Now the full story, year by year. In 1980, about one adult in seven was underweight.

Obesity was rarer, about one in twenty. But it climbed, year after year.

Around 2007, the two lines crossed. Since then, obesity is the more common of the two.

Part of that is good news: fewer adults are too thin, because fewer go hungry.

And obesity kept climbing: nearly tripled since 1990, to about one adult in six by 2024.

So the world's weight problem has flipped: from too little food, to too much.
