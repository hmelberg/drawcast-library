Take a breath. What is that air actually made of?

How is the air split? Drag the dividers to share out the pie.

Nitrogen is the giant: seventy-eight percent of every breath.

And it goes straight back out again: our bodies can't use it at all.

Oxygen, the gas we actually need, is only about a fifth.

Nearly all the rest is argon, a gas so unreactive that its name comes from the Greek for lazy.

And carbon dioxide, the gas behind global warming?

About 0.04 percent: four molecules in every ten thousand.

Tiny, yet that share has grown by half since before the industrial age.
