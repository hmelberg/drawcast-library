What is the universe made of?

What is the universe made of? The biggest share is dark energy, which makes space expand faster and faster.

Next comes dark matter: unseen stuff whose gravity holds the galaxies together.

And everything else, every star, planet and person: how big a share is that? Drag its edge.

Every atom on the periodic table fits in that thin wedge: every star, every planet, and you.

Dark matter is about five times as much as all of that.

We know it is there because galaxies spin too fast to hold together on the gravity of what we can see.

And dark energy is about two thirds of everything.

It turned up in 1998, when distant exploding stars looked dimmer than expected: the expansion is speeding up.

The shares come from the Planck satellite, which mapped the oldest light in the universe.

So about ninety-five percent of the universe is something nobody has yet identified.
