How many nuclear warheads are left in the world today?

The first bombs were built in 1945. By 1986, the world's stockpiles held about seventy thousand.

Draw the rest of the line, to 2025.

About twelve thousand. Five of every six warheads are gone.

So the end of the Cold War did that?

Mostly. Washington and Moscow agreed to take their arsenals apart, year after year.

But the cuts have slowed to a crawl: only about a thousand fewer since 2020.

And China is going the other way. Its stockpile has doubled since 2019, to about six hundred.

Here's one for you, though. One country took its count all the way to zero.

South Africa built six bombs in the 1980s, in secret. Then it took every one apart.

The only country ever to give up nuclear weapons it built itself.
