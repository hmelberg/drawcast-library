Two pumps in one heart

How blood runs a figure of eight through heart, lungs and body, and why the left side pushes five times harder.

How does blood travel through the heart and lungs? It runs two loops: one to the lungs to pick up oxygen, one to the body to deliver it.

The heart sits between them: four chambers, with a solid wall down the middle that makes it two pumps.

The heart's right side is drawn on your left, as if it faces you. It handles oxygen-poor blood, shown blue.

The left side handles oxygen-rich blood, shown red. The wall keeps the two apart.

Used blood comes back from the body through the great veins, the venae cavae, into the right atrium, a holding chamber.

It passes through a valve into the right ventricle, the pump.

That sends it up the pulmonary artery to the lungs.

It counts as an artery because it leaves the heart, even though its blood is oxygen-poor.

In the lungs the blood drops carbon dioxide and picks up oxygen.

Then it returns, now red, through the pulmonary veins to the left atrium.

From there it fills the left ventricle.

That drives it out through the aorta to every organ, and back round to the right side again.

A figure of eight.

At rest each side moves about five litres a minute, roughly all the blood you have.

Same volume, very different push. The left ventricle drives blood round the whole body at about 120 millimetres of mercury.

The right needs only about 25 for the lungs. Their vessels are short and delicate, and high pressure would flood them with fluid.

So the left ventricle's wall grows about three times thicker than the right's.

Why two pumps at all? A fish has one: it pumps blood through its gills and on to the body, and most of the push is lost in the gills.

Our second pump gives blood a fresh, strong push after the lungs. Two pumps side by side: equal in volume, unequal in force.

The right ventricle pumps five litres a minute. How much does the left pump?

About five litres: every drop passes through both loops in turn, so both sides move the same volume — the left just pushes harder.
