Read Russian in 14 letters

Fourteen Cyrillic letters, a handful of words and a short Russian text you can read by the end, learned the way memory works best.

Can you learn to read Russian in a few minutes? Here is the test: by the end, you'll read this.

Most English speakers see this word and read it as "pectopah".

That mistake shows the way in: many of these shapes you already know — and some of them lie.

Russian has thirty-three letters, but we'll learn only fourteen of the commonest: together they fill about three letters in every four on a page.

We'll go letters, then words, then the text — and you'll test yourself at each step, because pulling a thing from memory fixes it far better than reading it again.

First, five true friends: A, K, M, O and T look and sound just as in English.

With only those, try reading a word.

How does КОТ sound?

It's кот, kot: every letter a true friend.

And кот means cat; мама is mum, and там means there.

Next come the false friends: letters that look English but say something else.

В says v, Е says ye, Н says n, Р is a rolled r, С says s, and У says oo.

Now go back to the word that fooled you.

How does РЕСТОРАН sound?

It's ресторан, restoran: a restaurant, once the liars say their real sounds.

And this one, which looks like "het", is нет: no.

Last, three new faces — and a new shape sticks best with a picture or a story attached.

Д looks like a little house, and it says d, as in дом: house.

И looks like a backwards N; it says ee, and on its own it means "and".

П is the Greek pi, and says p, as in суп: soup.

One more, with a letter from each row: вода, water. Listen: an O without the stress sounds like a.

Type the word for house.

дом: house. Д, the little house, then О and М.

Before the text, pull the words back from memory. Match each Russian word to its meaning.

Now the test from the start, with the letters put away: read it yourself first.

Where is the cat?

At home: НЕТ, no, then КОТ ДОМА, the cat at home.

Мама в ресторане: word for word, mum in restaurant; the extra e comes with "in", and Russian needs no "is" and no "the".

Там суп и вода: there's soup and water.

А кот? Нет, кот дома: and the cat? No, the cat is at home.

One honest caveat: this is print, and Russian handwriting looks quite different; that's another small lesson.

So you didn't learn all of Russian: you learned fourteen common letters, leaned on the ones you knew, named the liars, and tested yourself. That was enough to read.

A word you've never seen: how does ТРАНСПОРТ sound?

It's транспорт, transport: Р, Н and С are the liars, П is pi.

And pectopah? It was a restaurant all along. The other nineteen letters fall the same way.
