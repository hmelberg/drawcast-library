Which line is longer?

Two lines, one looks longer: tap the longer one, then see why the Müller-Lyer illusion fools almost everyone, even when you know the trick.

Here is a line with its fins flared out, like the feathers on an arrow's tail.

And here is a second one, with its ends folded in, like arrowheads.

Which line is longer? Tap it, or tap Same length.

They are exactly the same length.

Drop a straight edge from each end, and both lines start and stop at the same place.

Take the fins away, and the two lines are plainly twins.

Put them back, and the top one looks longer again, even though you now know better.

This is the Müller-Lyer illusion, drawn by the German Franz Müller-Lyer in 1889.

In the 1960s, researchers reported that people who grew up without square rooms, such as San foragers in the Kalahari, hardly saw it.

But a 2025 review found those studies had serious flaws. The illusion seems to come with our eyes and brains, not with our buildings.
