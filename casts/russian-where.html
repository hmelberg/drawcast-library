Where is it? где, в and на in Russian

Ask where something is in Russian with где, answer with тут, там, в and на, and see why the place word ends in е.

How do you say where something is in Russian? We'll follow this cat around a house and find out.

I'll ask the question: где кот? Где means where, so: where's the cat?

He's far off by the sea, so I answer кот там: the cat is there, with там from lesson one.

Now he runs in to me, and the answer changes: кот тут, the cat is here.

Тут is here, near the speaker; там is over there. And, as always, Russian needs no word for is.

To say where exactly, put в, in, before the place: кот в до́ме, the cat is in the house.

And дом has gained an е, just as ресторан did in the story: в рестора́не.

Где кот? Up on the table: кот на столе́.

На means on, and стол gains the same е: на столе́.

And now? Кот на окне́: on the window, on the sill.

Окно́ ends in о, so the о makes way for е: окно́, на окне́.

So here's the pattern: after в or на, most words end in е.

Где кот? Back where he started: кот на мо́ре, at the seaside.

Мо́ре already ends in е, so it doesn't change at all.

Why на? Russians say на мо́ре for a seaside holiday; в мо́ре would mean in the water.

The window: на окне́, from окно́.

The cat is at the seaside. Tap the sentence that says so.

Кот на мо́ре: на, and мо́ре stays as it is.

The cat is on the table. Finish it: Кот на …

Столе́: стол, plus the е that comes after на.

Кот на кни́ге: on, so на, and the а of кни́га makes way for е, as окно́'s о did.

So that's where anything is in Russian: где asks, в or на answers, and the place after them ends in е.
