Where the 206 bones are

A tour of the adult skeleton that tallies its 206 bones region by region, and shows why more than half sit in the hands and feet.

Where in your body are the 206 bones of an adult skeleton? Start at the top: the skull, with the lower jaw hinged beneath it.

Down the middle runs the spine: seven neck vertebrae, twelve in the chest, five in the lower back, then the sacrum.

From the chest vertebrae hang twelve pairs of ribs, and most of them meet in front at the breastbone, the sternum.

That central column, plus a few small bones in the ears and throat, is the axial skeleton: eighty bones that protect the brain, the spinal cord and the heart.

Keep a tally: eighty so far. Everything else hangs from this column.

The arms hang from a ring of four bones: two collarbones in front, two shoulder blades behind.

Below that, one bone in the upper arm, then two in the forearm, so the forearm can twist.

The legs hang from the pelvis in the same pattern: one thigh bone, then two in the lower leg, plus the kneecap.

Now the hands, where the count climbs fast: each one holds 27 bones.

Eight in the wrist, five in the palm, and fourteen in the fingers.

So the arms and shoulders together come to sixty-four.

Each foot holds 26, built on the same plan, so the hips and legs come to sixty-two.

Eighty, sixty-four and sixty-two make 206.

A newborn starts with around 300; many fuse as it grows. This sacrum began as five separate vertebrae.

Click on the bone where the ribs meet in front.

The sternum, the breastbone: the upper ribs join it through cartilage.

Roughly what share of the 206 bones sit in the hands and feet?

More than half: 27 in each hand and 26 in each foot make 106 of the 206.
