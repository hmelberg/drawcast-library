How hot are the world's hottest peppers?

Place four chillies on a ten-times-per-step heat scale, then learn why the burn is not real heat, and why birds do not feel it.

Chilli heat is measured in Scoville units, and a sweet bell pepper scores zero. Each step on this line is ten times hotter than the last.

How hot is each pepper? Drag each card to its place on the Scoville scale.

A jalapeño is a gentle few thousand units.

A habanero sits in the hundreds of thousands.

The Carolina Reaper averages about one point six million: as hot as some police pepper spray.

And since 2023 the record belongs to Pepper X, from the same grower, at about two point seven million.

Yet none of it is real heat. The burn comes from capsaicin, which switches on a heat sensor in your nerves called TRPV1.

Your brain is told you are on fire. David Julius used chilli to find that sensor, and shared the Nobel Prize for it in 2021.

Birds have a version of the sensor that capsaicin cannot switch on. So they eat chillies happily, and spread the seeds.

The burn may be the plant's way of choosing who gets to eat it.
