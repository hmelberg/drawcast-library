Why bubble sort is so slow

Watch bubble sort swap neighbours one pair at a time, count the swaps it must make, then sort six bars yourself and beat it.

Why is bubble sort so slow? Six bars show it. Its one rule: only neighbours may swap.

Five stands before two, so they swap.

Then it walks along the row. Five and eight are in order, but eight and one are not, so they swap too.

Eight and nine are fine; nine and three swap. One pass has carried the tallest bar to the end.

But each pass settles just one bar for sure, so it takes pass after pass.

So how many swaps in all? Go back to the starting row, and count the pairs that stand in the wrong order.

Five stands ahead of three smaller bars, two ahead of one, eight ahead of two, nine ahead of one: seven in all.

A neighbour swap fixes exactly one wrong pair and disturbs no other. So bubble sort makes exactly seven swaps; our first pass made three of them.

Worst case, a row in reverse: every pair is wrong. With n bars, that is n times n minus one, over two.

A thousand bars: half a million swaps. A million bars: five hundred billion.

Faster sorts, like merge sort, compare items far apart. A million items take them about twenty million steps instead.

No wonder Donald Knuth judged that bubble sort has nothing to recommend it, except a catchy name.

Now sort these six yourself, one neighbour swap at a time.

Sort the bars. Can you do it in seven swaps?

Seven swaps, one for each pair that stood in the wrong order.

Ten bars in reverse order. How many swaps?

45: all ten times nine over two pairs are in the wrong order, and each swap fixes just one.
