On the Moon, which lands first: a hammer or a feather?

Drop a hammer and a feather on the Moon: which lands first? Guess, then watch Apollo 15's famous test prove Galileo right.

On the Moon, an astronaut drops a hammer and a feather at the same moment. Which one lands first?

The hammer weighs over a kilo. The feather, about thirty grams.

On the Moon, which lands first?

They land together. With no air to hold the feather back, gravity speeds both up at exactly the same rate.

Astronaut David Scott tried it on Apollo 15, in 1971, live on television.

Side by side, they hit the dust together.

'How about that,' said Scott. 'Mr. Galileo was correct in his findings.'

Galileo had argued, more than three centuries earlier, that without air, heavy and light things fall alike.

On Earth, the air pushes back on the feather's broad, light vanes, so it drifts while the hammer drops.

And it was a falcon's feather, a nod to their lunar module, which was called Falcon.
