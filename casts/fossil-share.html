How much of the world's energy still comes from fossil fuels?

Two hosts argue over the fossil slice of the world's energy pie, then find why it has barely shrunk since 1990 even as solar power boomed.

How much of the world's energy still comes from coal, oil and gas? Count everything: power plants, factories, cars, planes and heating.

Surely not that much any more. There are solar panels everywhere. Half?

Drag the edge of the slice to your guess.

Eighty-six percent? In 2025? That can't be right.

It is. In 1990 it was ninety, so in thirty-five years the fossil slice shrank by just four points.

And that's despite a solar boom: solar power has grown about eighty-five times over since 2010.

So clean energy is winning?

Not yet. Look at the size of the pies: the world now uses about eighty percent more energy than in 1990.

So a slightly smaller slice of a much bigger pie is still more fossil fuel: about seventy percent more.

So clean energy has mostly fed the new demand, not replaced coal and oil.

Exactly. That's the real race: clean energy has to grow faster than demand.
