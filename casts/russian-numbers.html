How do you count to ten in Russian?

Оди́н, два, три.

Оди́н is one, and два and три are old relatives of duo and trio.

Четы́ре, пять, шесть.

Пять is a cousin of penta, five; and шесть, six, starts with Ш, the sh you heard in хорошо́.

Семь, во́семь, де́вять, де́сять.

Семь, seven, hides inside во́семь, eight; and nine and ten differ by one letter: в in де́вять, с in де́сять.

Now look at the ends: one to four have nothing special, but five to ten all end in the same letter.

It's the soft sign: it makes no sound of its own, it only softens the letter before it.

The digits are gone: can you still read the words? Which one is eight? Tap it.

Во́семь, eight: the one with семь inside.

Ten numbers are enough for a phone number, and Masha reads hers out one digit at a time.

Пять, три, де́вять, два, семь, во́семь, шесть.

Russians often say a number in bigger chunks, like five hundred and thirty-nine, but digit by digit, everyone understands you.

Numbers also find you a seat: back in Masha's café, she shows me to my table.

Стол is the table from lesson two, and но́мер means number; I said the number back to check I'd heard it right.

So ten short words, several with cousins in English, and a letter you see in six of them but never hear.

Five questions now, from memory, then your score. Match each Russian number to its digit.

Type three in Russian.

Три: three, like trio.

What number is во́семь? Type it in digits.

Во́семь is eight: семь, seven, hides inside it.

My number starts пять, три, де́вять. Type those three digits.

Пять, три, де́вять: five, three, nine, the start of my number.

Which one is 10?

Де́сять: ten has с in the middle; де́вять, with в, is nine.
