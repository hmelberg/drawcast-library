What does a positive test mean?

Guess how many of 108 positive tests are real, and see why a 90 % accurate test is right only about one time in twelve.

If a test is ninety percent accurate and you test positive, how likely are you to be sick? Say one person in a hundred has the disease.

And the test is right nine times in ten: it finds nine in ten of the sick, and it clears nine in ten of the healthy.

Now test a thousand people. A hundred and eight of them test positive.

Of these hundred and eight, how many do you think really have the disease? Mark how many are sick.

Of the thousand, only ten have the disease, and the test finds nine of them.

But nine hundred and ninety are healthy, and one in ten of them is flagged anyway: ninety-nine false alarms.

So a positive result means about an eight percent chance of having the disease, not ninety. That is why a positive screen is usually followed by a second test.
