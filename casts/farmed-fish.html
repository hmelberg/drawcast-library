How much of the world's seafood is farmed?

Draw how fish farming grew after 1990 beside the wild catch, then see why it took over: the sea's catch hit a ceiling decades ago.

How much of the world's seafood is farmed, rather than caught in the wild? The blue line is the wild catch, in million tonnes a year.

Red is farmed: fish, shellfish and seaweed. In 1990, farms made seventeen million tonnes, a fifth as much as the wild catch.

Draw the farmed line on to 2024.

In 2013 the farms passed the wild catch, and by 2024 they made about a hundred and forty million tonnes.

And look at the blue line: the wild catch has been stuck at around ninety million tonnes since the mid-1990s.

The seas were fished to their limit, so all the growth since has come from farms.

That farmed total counts mussels, shrimp and seaweed too: sixty percent of everything.

Counting animals alone, farms passed the half-way mark in 2022.

So the picture of seafood as a boat hauling in a net is out of date: most of it now grows up in a farm.
