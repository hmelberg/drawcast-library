How fast can they run?

Place a cheetah, a racehorse, the fastest human and an elephant on a speed line, and see how far apart their top speeds really are.

How fast can animals, and people, run? Here are four runners: a cheetah, a racehorse, the fastest man ever timed, and an elephant.

Where does each runner's top speed lie? Drag each card onto the line.

The cheetah is the fastest runner on land: one was timed at about a hundred and four kilometres an hour, over two hundred metres.

A racehorse comes next, though a long way behind. The record, set in 2008, is about seventy-one kilometres an hour over a quarter mile.

Then us. Usain Bolt peaked at about forty-four kilometres an hour during his world record in 2009.

And the elephant is not as slow as it looks: Asian elephants have been timed at twenty-five kilometres an hour, more than twice the pace of an easy jog.

So a cheetah runs more than twice as fast as the fastest person who ever lived.
